01 · Work · The AI employees

From one to four

A team of AI employees

I grew one autonomous AI employee into a team of four. Each runs on its own Mac, with its own Slack channel, mailbox and Claude account. They do go-to-market work, the first on veterans outreach and the second on health-system sales, and three contracts are now in progress toward signing.

One continuous mind

A harness around Claude Code

Each employee is a single headless Claude Code session. My harness pins it and resumes it for every Slack message, email, scheduled wake and heartbeat. The off-the-shelf product it replaced had split one agent into 126 sessions.

Its memory is a pyramid, inspired by OpenClaw:

  1. Distillation promotes lasting directives on its own
  2. Versioned trimming
  3. A structure-aware seed

The employees wrote much of the harness themselves. One of them built the memory pyramid and the interrupt model, in pull requests I reviewed.

02 · Research

Independent · July 2026

PeregrineX: adversarial safety testing of a neural flight controller

I built a safety-qualification harness and flew a paired 800-flight adversarial campaign against mc_nn_control, the TensorFlow Lite neural position controller in PX4-Autopilot mainline, and its classical counterpart. Pass/fail rates matched (27.0% vs 24.5%). How they failed did not.

A Gaussian-process-guided search then mapped the safety boundary more sample-efficiently than uniform sampling, validated across three surrogate model families on a 2,000-run ArduPilot baseline.

Concussions in Plain Sight

MIT Sloan Sports Analytics Conference 2027 submission

A pre-registered event study over 17 NFL seasons, with a sealed 2024–25 holdout, that finds unreported concussions in public play-by-play data. 23% of concussed quarterbacks finished the game, and their play fell by 0.33 EPA per dropback after the hit compared with players who were hit but not diagnosed (p < 0.001).
